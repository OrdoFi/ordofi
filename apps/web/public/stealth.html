Stealth locked

Every transfer on Robinhood Chain is public forever. Hand someone your address to be paid at and you hand them your balance and your entire payment history. Stealth addresses fix exactly that. You publish one meta-address; anyone paying you derives a brand new address from it that only you can spend from. The payments stay public — they just stop being linkable to you.

Your stealth account lives in your wallet

Two keys are derived from a single signature: a viewing key that finds payments addressed to you, and a spending key that moves them. Nothing is stored on our servers and nothing is written to this device — sign again on any browser and you get the same account back.

Signing is free and costs no gas. It never authorises a transfer.

Requires a wallet on Robinhood Chain.

      How it works

      01 · PUBLISH

      One meta-address

      Your wallet signs once. From that signature come a spending key and a viewing key, and from those a meta-address: st:rho:0x…. It is two public keys and nothing else, so publishing it gives away nothing.

      02 · DERIVE

      A fresh address per payment

      The payer rolls a random ephemeral key, combines it with your viewing key to get a shared secret, and adds that secret to your spending key. The result is an address nobody has ever used, which only you hold the key to.

      03 · ANNOUNCE

      A public breadcrumb only you can read

      The payer publishes their ephemeral public key to the canonical ERC-5564 announcer. It tells the world nothing; it tells you, and only you, where to look. A one-byte view tag lets your browser skip 255 of every 256 announcements.

      04 · SPEND

      Yours, whenever

      Your browser recomputes the same secret with your viewing key, matches the address, and derives the spending key locally. Withdrawals are signed on your device and submitted through OrdoFi's own RPC, straight to the sequencer.

      What this is not. It is not a mixer and there is no anonymity set: no funds are pooled and nothing of yours is ever mingled with anyone else's. The sender remains fully visible — their wallet pays the announcement and the transfer. What is hidden is the recipient: nobody can tell that a stealth address belongs to you, so publishing one meta-address no longer publishes your balance and your history. Withdrawing several stealth payments into the same address links them back together, so withdraw to somewhere fresh if that matters to you. Keys are derived from a wallet signature and held in this tab only — close it and they are gone, sign again and they come back.
